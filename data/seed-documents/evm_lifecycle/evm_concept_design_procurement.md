# EVM Concept, Design & Procurement

## Historical Development

The journey of Electronic Voting Machines (EVMs) in India:

- **1977**: Election Commission first considers electronic voting alternatives
- **1979**: First EVM prototype developed by Electronics Corporation of India Limited (ECIL)
- **1982**: First field trial in Paravur constituency, Kerala (50 polling stations)
- **1989**: Legal backing provided through amendment to Section 61A of the Representation of the People Act, 1951
- **1998-99**: EVMs used in 16 State Assembly elections
- **2004**: First complete general election conducted using EVMs nationwide
- **2006**: M2 model introduced with enhanced security features
- **2013**: M3 model introduced with One-Time Programmable (OTP) chips and tamper detection

## Design Specifications

### Core Design Principles
1. **Standalone Operation**: No network connectivity — eliminates remote hacking possibility
2. **Battery Powered**: Operates on a single 7.5V alkaline battery — no dependency on power grid
3. **One-Time Programmable (OTP)**: Software burned into chip during manufacturing — cannot be altered
4. **Tamper Detection**: Self-destruct mechanism if unauthorized access is attempted
5. **Simplicity**: Single-button interface for voters — reduces errors

### Manufacturing Standards
- **ISO 9001:2015** certified manufacturing facilities
- **BIS standards** compliance for electronic components
- Each EVM undergoes **42-point quality check** during manufacturing
- **Environmental testing**: Temperature (-10°C to +55°C), humidity (10% to 90%), altitude (up to 4,000m)
- **Vibration testing** for transportation resilience
- **Shelf life**: 15 years for M3 machines

## Procurement Process

### Government-to-Government (G2G) Model
- **No private sector involvement** at any stage
- Orders placed directly with BEL (Bangalore) and ECIL (Hyderabad)
- Both are Public Sector Undertakings (PSUs) under Government of India
- **Cost per unit**: Approximately ₹17,000 for Control Unit + Balloting Unit set (2023 prices)
- **VVPAT unit cost**: Approximately ₹16,500 per unit

### Production Capacity
- BEL and ECIL together produce approximately **5 lakh units annually**
- Total inventory maintained: ~25 lakh Control Units, ~25 lakh Balloting Units
- **Buffer stock**: 20% above requirement maintained at all times

### Quality Assurance During Procurement
1. Factory inspection by ECI-appointed technical experts
2. Random sampling and testing of each production batch
3. Software verification against approved source code
4. Firmware hash verification
5. Final acceptance testing before dispatch to state warehouses
