# Election Timeline — Five Phases

## Phase 1: Electoral Roll Preparation

### Continuous Registration
- Electoral rolls are updated continuously throughout the year
- Special summary revision conducted annually (typically October-November)
- Qualifying date: **January 1st** of the year of revision (changed from October 1st)
- New voters turning 18 on four qualifying dates per year: January 1, April 1, July 1, October 1

### Activities
- Booth Level Officers (BLOs) conduct door-to-door verification
- Additions, deletions, and corrections processed
- Draft rolls published for public inspection and claims/objections
- Final electoral rolls published before election notification

## Phase 2: Election Announcement & MCC

### Announcement
- The Election Commission announces election dates at a press conference
- **Model Code of Conduct (MCC) comes into effect immediately** upon announcement
- ECI announces: (a) dates for filing nominations, (b) date of scrutiny, (c) last date for withdrawal, (d) date of poll, (e) date of counting

### Timeline (Typical for Lok Sabha)
- Announcement to first phase of polling: Approximately **3-4 weeks**
- Entire election process from announcement to results: **6-8 weeks** (for multi-phase elections)

## Phase 3: Nominations & Scrutiny

### Nomination Filing
- Candidates file nomination papers with the Returning Officer during the designated period
- **Security deposit**: Rs. 25,000 for Lok Sabha (Rs. 12,500 for SC/ST), Rs. 10,000 for Assembly (Rs. 5,000 for SC/ST)
- Must submit **Form 26 affidavit** disclosing criminal cases, assets, liabilities, and education

### Scrutiny
- Returning Officer examines all nomination papers on the date fixed for scrutiny
- Candidates may be rejected for: incomplete forms, insufficient deposit, disqualification under law
- **Period for withdrawal**: After scrutiny, candidates have a few days to withdraw nominations

### Symbol Allotment
- Recognized national and state party candidates get their reserved symbols
- Independent candidates choose from a list of free symbols provided by the ECI
- Final list of contesting candidates published after withdrawal deadline

## Phase 4: Campaigning & Silence Period

### Campaigning (Typically 2-3 weeks)
- Political parties and candidates hold public rallies, roadshows, door-to-door canvassing
- Campaign expenditure monitored by Election Expenditure Observers
- Media coverage monitored for fairness and balance by MCMC (Media Certification and Monitoring Committee)
- Paid news and surrogate advertisements prohibited

### Silence Period
- **48 hours before the close of polling**: All campaigning must stop
- No public meetings, rallies, or processions
- No canvassing by any means (including social media appeals for votes)
- Violation can lead to criminal prosecution under Section 126 of RP Act

## Phase 5: Polling & Counting

### Polling Day
- Voting hours: Typically **7:00 AM to 6:00 PM** (may vary by region)
- Mock poll conducted at every polling station before 7 AM
- Polling conducted using EVMs with VVPAT
- Voters who are in the queue at closing time are allowed to vote
- Separate queues for women and senior citizens

### Counting Day
- Usually 3-4 days after polling (or later for multi-phase elections)
- Counting conducted at designated counting centers
- Multiple counting tables with counting supervisors and counting agents
- VVPAT verification in 5 randomly selected polling stations per constituency
- Results announced round by round
- Final result declared by the Returning Officer in Form 20
- Election Commission compiles nationwide results
