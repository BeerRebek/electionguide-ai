# Model Code of Conduct (MCC)

## When Does MCC Apply?
- Comes into effect from the **date of announcement of elections** (not the notification date)
- Remains in force until the **completion of the election process** (results declared)
- Applies to all constituencies going to polls

## Part I — General Conduct
1. No party or candidate shall indulge in any activity prejudicial to the unity and integrity of the country
2. No appeal to caste or communal feelings for securing votes
3. No use of temples, mosques, churches, or other places of worship for election propaganda
4. All parties shall avoid "corrupt practices" and offences under election law
5. Criticism of other parties must be confined to their policies and programs — no personal attacks on private life

## Part II — Meetings and Processions
1. Prior permission from police/local authority required for public meetings
2. Processions must follow prescribed routes
3. Loudspeakers and music systems subject to local regulations and time limits
4. **48-hour silence period**: No public meetings or processions during the last 48 hours before poll closing

## Part III — Polling Day
1. All workers must carry identity cards issued by the party
2. No canvassing within **100 meters** of a polling station
3. Voters shall not be transported to polling stations by parties (except for physically disabled voters)
4. No liquor distribution on polling day and 48 hours before
5. No intimidation of voters

## Part IV — Party in Power
1. **No use of government vehicles, aircraft, or personnel** for campaigning
2. **No policy announcements**: The government shall not announce any new policies, projects, or schemes after the MCC comes into force
3. **No ad hoc appointments** in government services that may influence voters
4. **No laying of foundation stones** or inaugurations of public works
5. **Equal access**: All parties must get equal access to government-owned media
6. **Government advertisements**: No advertisements at the cost of the public exchequer highlighting achievements of the government

## Part V — Election Expenditure
- Expenditure limits are set by the ECI for each type of election
- **Lok Sabha**: Rs. 95 lakh per candidate (as of 2022 revision)
- **State Assembly**: Rs. 40 lakh per candidate (varies by state)
- Candidates must maintain a **day-to-day account** of all expenditure
- Expenditure includes travel, meetings, advertisements, and any other election-related spending

## Enforcement
- The ECI has constitutional authority under Article 324 to enforce the MCC
- Violations reported through **cVIGIL app** (citizens can upload photos/videos of violations)
- Flying squads and surveillance teams monitor compliance
- The ECI can issue:
  - Advisory/warning letters
  - Notices seeking explanation
  - FIRs under IPC sections
  - Ban on campaigning for specific periods
  - In extreme cases, postponement of poll or countermanding
